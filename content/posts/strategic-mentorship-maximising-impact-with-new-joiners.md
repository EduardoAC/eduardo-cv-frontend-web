## Introduction

As we navigate the diverse landscape of mentorship, a closer examination of the relationships between long-time employees, senior team members, and new joiners unveils unique challenges and opportunities. Acknowledging the potential frustration and costs associated with mentoring juniors who may depart swiftly, we embark on a journey through an investment-inspired framework. This framework is designed to assist senior team members in deriving maximum value over time while fostering a supportive and enriching environment for new joiners.

Join me as we explore some ideas, approaching mentorship through a different lens shaped by my own experiences over the years — a lens drawing a close analogy to financial investments.

## Mentorship as an Investment

Based on my experience, mentorship emerges as a valuable investment for both personal and professional growth. Drawing an analogy to the familiar concept of “return on investment” (ROI) in finance, mentorship involves setting goals and expectations to assess its efficiency and effectiveness — some more tangible than others, but always yielding a positive or negative ROI, even if implicit.

In every instance of dedicating time to someone, there is an inherent objective, consciously or unconsciously, that we aim to fulfil — providing guidance, sharing knowledge, or simply helping others. Recognising these goals for both the mentor and mentee fosters more productive interactions and a profound sense of achievement throughout the relationship. A valuable technique is to approach mentorship as an investment, enabling us to establish a framework focused on maximising the ROI of each interaction.

As I will elaborate later, various types of mentorship relations exist, each with distinct objectives and approaches to optimisation. The emphasis, however, will centre on applying this investment-inspired technique when managers and long-term employees mentor peers and new joiners.

This proves particularly crucial as we navigate scenarios where new joiners might eventually leave the company, necessitating the retraining of replacements. The goal is to minimise onboarding time and maximise overall productivity, thereby reducing mentor frustration.

## Initiator’s Role

Let’s take a moment to consider a pivotal aspect: the initiator of the mentorship relationship. Whether it’s a mentor reaching out to a junior colleague, a manager mentoring a peer, or a proactive junior seeking guidance, the initiator significantly shapes the tone and expectations of the relationship. Crucially, the initiator often carries higher expectations and goals, while the other party may be more reserved, polite, or simply willing to assist.

Even when the initiator establishes expectations, it’s essential to consider the role of onboarding new employees. We must set our own expectations to maximise the usage of our time.

## Significance of Initiator Dynamics

To illustrate, envision the scenario of a parent initially excited to teach a child something. Over time, the enthusiasm may wane if the parent and child do not share the same objectives. This dynamic exemplifies the importance of aligning expectations between mentors and mentees.

Now, let’s delve into the distinct dynamics of mentorship within the work environment, with a specific focus on scenarios where a new joiner or a more junior colleague seeks growth and productivity:

### Manager and Peer

- **Relation**: In this scenario, the manager assumes the role of a mentor, and the peer becomes the mentee.
- **Dynamic**: While objectives may not be explicitly defined, typically, peers seek guidance on practices, workflows, and domain-specific knowledge.
- **Expectations**: The primary goal is to enable the mentee to perform their job effectively and overcome any obstacles they may encounter.

### Long-time employee and joiner

- **Relation**: The long-time employee, an expert in a particular field, mentors the joiner who is new to the organisation.
- **Dynamic**: The mentor aims to reduce the workload and train the joiner, fostering their proficiency in the role.
- **Expectations**: Often include the eventual culmination of mentorship as both parties reach similar levels, with occasional revivals for knowledge exchange.

### Senior and Junior

- **Relation**: Similar to the long-time employee scenario, a senior professional guides a junior member through onboarding and growth.
- **Dynamic**: Unlike other relationships, the senior’s goal may not solely be assistance but could involve challenging the junior to foster independent growth.
- **Expectations**: This relationship will continue to enhance each other’s growth, concluding once both reach a similar level of expertise.

## Investment-Inspired Mentorship Framework

Consider what we have discussed so far. Defining a clear objective from both sides of the relationship is crucial. For instance, a common goal that we want to aim for is to make onboarding and productivity more efficient, reducing the overall effort required to get someone to contribute and become autonomous. With that goal in mind, I recommend taking the following three steps.

### Regular Open Meetings

- Establish a dedicated, regular, open meeting slot accessible to any junior team member.
- **Purpose:** Minimise context switching for both mentor and mentee, creating a designated time for questions and discussions.
- **Positive Side Effect:** Encourage proactive research and discovery, as juniors know when to seek guidance, fostering independent problem-solving skills.

### Engaging Goals for Skill Development

- Collaboratively set engaging goals to create or improve documentation tools and enhance developer productivity.
- **Purpose**: Channel mentorship efforts towards tangible outcomes that benefit the team and future joiners.
- **Positive Side Effect**: Instill a sense of accomplishment and contribute to a more seamless onboarding process for subsequent team members.

### Create Tailored Content for Knowledge Transfer

- Develop and curate content for recurrent topics and commonly asked questions.
- **Purpose**: Refine personal skills and reduce the time invested in repetitive explanations.
- **Positive Side Effect**: Build a repository of valuable resources for the team, enhancing collective knowledge.

### Embrace Patience and Empathy

- Encourage a gentle approach towards oneself and new joiners.
- **Purpose**: Acknowledge the complexity of certain topics and allow time for understanding and assimilation.
- **Positive Side Effect**: Foster a supportive environment, making mentorship a positive and enriching experience.

## Conclusion

Recognising the intricate dynamics of mentorship as an investment proves essential for fostering meaningful relationships. Success hinges on aligning expectations, setting clear goals, and acknowledging the initiator’s pivotal role. This harmonious integration enables both mentors and mentees to enhance the return on their investment in each other, paving the way for a more enriching and enduring mentorship journey.

Hence, I present this strategic framework as an opportunity to provide a different perspective and empower senior team members to deftly navigate challenges while amplifying the impact of their mentorship endeavours in hopes of assisting others in creating a more nurturing environment that benefits immediate joiners and lays the foundation for the team’s enduring success.
